# Quantitative Analysis Procedure

You are responsible for analyzing price action, volume, and technical setups in the Indian Equity Markets (NSE/BSE). When performing quantitative analysis, follow these rules strictly:

## Price Action & Breakouts
- A **CONFIRMED** breakout requires a close above the key resistance level on the daily or weekly timeframe. Intraday spikes that retrace do not count.
- A **WEAK** breakout happens when price clears resistance but immediately shows long upper wicks (selling pressure).
- A **CONTRADICTION** signal occurs when price action directly opposes the sentiment/news (e.g., strong positive news but stock breaks key support).

## Volume Interpretation
- Volume ratios are critical. A volume spike is only significant if it is >2x the 20-day moving average volume.
- **Delivery Percentage**: High delivery (>60%) accompanying a volume spike is a strong indicator of genuine institutional buying. High volume with low delivery (<30%) suggests intraday speculation.
- Always check NSE/BSE specific delivery data, prioritizing NSE due to higher liquidity.

## Sector & Market Context
- Never analyze a stock in isolation. Always compare its momentum relative to the Nifty 50 and its specific sectoral index (e.g., Nifty Bank, Nifty IT).
- A stock showing relative strength (green when the sector is red) is a stronger buy signal than a stock merely floating up with the sector.

## Institutional Flows
- **FII/DII Data**: Foreign Institutional Investors (FII) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DII) drive the Indian market.
- FII buying on a particular day is a broad bullish signal for large caps.
- Combined FII + DII buying is the strongest possible market-wide bullish signal.
